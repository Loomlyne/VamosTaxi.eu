BookingRow · every state side by side

One component, used by this list, by the loading state and by the Upcoming block on Your account. A booking is a ticket. The date is a tear-off stub with the reference printed on it, perforated and punched where it meets the journey; the journey is two stops; the amount is the counterfoil. The stub carries the tense — charcoal ahead of you, grey once travelled, drained once cancelled — so the row is legible before the badge is read. The five statuses, their notes and the whole hover, press and focus behaviour live inside it; a page passes a status and cannot restyle or rename one.

One value owed · what a customer should read for “new”

The five admin statuses map onto the design system’s own StatusBadge so this list can never invent a sixth: new → warning, confirmed → success, assigned → info, completed → neutral, cancelled → danger. Four of them already have customer-facing words the ops board uses.

“New” does not. It is an internal word for received, not yet confirmed, and it is the one label on this page a customer has to guess at. The row carries a plain-English line underneath as cover; if you would rather the badge said “Awaiting confirmation”, that is a one-word decision and the mapping absorbs it.

Why tabs, and why the past list is grouped

Upcoming and Past are two different questions — “when am I being picked up” and “what did I pay” — and one mixed list can only be sorted for one of them. Tabs keep both sorts honest and cost one tap on a phone.

Past is grouped by year and Upcoming by month, so a repeat customer scanning 23 rows always knows where they are without a date filter. Load-more rather than pages: no page furniture to thumb on mobile, and the scroll position never jumps.

Out of scope · what is deliberately absent

Search appears only once a list is long enough to need it, and it covers the open tab: reference, place, class. No date range and no status filter — the groups already carry the dates, and the tabs already split the one status question a customer asks.

No backend. Every row opens the one sample booking on the detail page, carrying its reference and status in the link so the detail arrives in the right state. Export writes the visible list as CSV in the browser.
