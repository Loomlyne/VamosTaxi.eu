<the_three_source_rule>
This is the governing rule of the whole task, and the one way it can fail badly. Stream 3 says
every item carries a recommended default "where one is defensible". The project rules forbid
inventing a value. Both are true at once, and the distinction is the source.

**A recommendation may be offered only when it comes from one of three sources, and the source
is named inline in the same block as the recommendation:**

1. **A law or regulation** — the instrument is cited, not a number pulled from the air. A
   data-subject-request response window is fixed by the GDPR and its Swiss counterpart; a
   finance-record retention period is fixed by the Swiss Code of Obligations. Write the period
   in the instrument's own units and name the instrument. If no instrument genuinely sets the
   period, this source does not apply — do not stretch it.
2. **The platform being built** — facts about our own implementation. The two payment-processor
   cookies already printed on the cookies page are the only real cookie names in existence today;
   session length, consent-cookie lifetime, link and code expiries, and the database region are
   our choices, already made. These we know and can state.
3. **An answer already given** — the 24-hour cancellation boundary and the three refund shares
   (owner decision 2) · the single support address (decision 4) · Zürich as the court of venue
   (decision 7) · three vehicle classes with the Van capacity confirmed (decision 13) · the
   accepted payment list with PayPal out (decision 5, as scoped) · carrier rather than
   intermediary (decision 1) · chat as a WhatsApp deep link (decision 11) · flight autofill in
   and hourly mode out (decisions 14 and 15) · and the seven engineering decision records from
   Stream 2.

**No recommendation may EVER be offered for these. Each reads "only you can answer this", and
the value stays blank:**

- Any money of any kind — fares, per-kilometre rates, surcharges, fees, the extra-stop fee, the
  city-stay fee, the oversize-item fee, the charge for waiting beyond the free allowance.
- The refund share when our own driver fails to show.
- The free airport waiting allowance and the free non-airport waiting allowance. The checklist
  records both as unverified marketing claims; the old site's figures are evidence of what the
  old page said and nothing more.
- Business-class passenger and bag capacity.
- The registered company name, street, postcode, UID, supervisory authority and licence.
- Insurance details, the dispute-resolution body, and the photography, brand-agency and
  build-agency credits.
- The seat count that makes a vehicle "large", and that class's cancellation window.
- How long before pickup the driver's name, plate and number arrive.
- Support hours, chat hours, response-time commitments, and the window for filing a complaint.
- Anything else still sitting in a labelled gap that the three sources above do not cover.

**Old-site figures.** Wayback captures dated 2024-07-14 to 2024-09-13 of a site that is now
offline. A figure from them may appear **only** in a line that says, in that same line, that this
is what the old site said — never in a recommendation. The document must state in its own words
that the old site's number is not evidence the number is right.
</the_three_source_rule>

<document_shape>
Section order is fixed. The launch summary is first because it is the part that gets read.

1. **Title and how to use this** — three or four lines under the document title, with **no
   second-level heading of their own**, so the launch summary is the first `##` in the file. What
   it is, that a blank is a decision rather than a delay, and that "I don't know yet" written in
   the blank is more useful than an empty line.
2. **What blocks launch** — the one-page summary, and the first `##` heading in the document.
   Six items as a plain numbered list, one line each plus one line of why it blocks. Nothing else
   on the page. These use an ordinary `1.` list marker, **not** the bold question-block prefix
   below, because they are pointers into the questions rather than questions themselves.
3. **Your company details**
4. **Your prices**
5. **Your policies and promises**
6. **How we handle your customers' data**
7. **Your photos** — the shot list
8. **Your accounts and links**
9. **Three things only you can decide**
10. **Quick confirmations** — one word each
11. **What we are handling ourselves** — so the owner sees what is off their plate, and can object
12. **What needs translating once you answer**
13. **Internal appendix — cross-reference** (marked internal, last, so the send is a truncation)

**Question block shape.** One running number across the whole document, 1..N, so the appendix can
point at a number. Every numbered block — question or photo slot — opens with the bold prefix
`**N.` at the start of a line, and the ten photo slots continue the same running number rather
than restarting at one. Every question is the same five-line block, and every line is present even
when the answer is short:

```
**7. How long does the driver wait at the airport before waiting becomes chargeable, and what
does it cost after that?**

- **Our recommendation:** only you can answer this — it is a promise your drivers have to keep.
- **What your old site said:** the old site said one hour. That is what the old page claimed; it
  is not evidence the figure is right.
- **If you leave this blank:** four pages show a labelled gap where the allowance should be, and
  we cannot state a waiting allowance anywhere on the site.
- **Needs translating once given:** yes — short phrase, four languages.
- **Your answer:**
```

Where there is no old-site figure, that line reads that the old site never published one — the
line is not dropped, because a missing line reads as an oversight.

**Voice.** This is the one artifact in this session that goes to the client, so voice matters as
much as accuracy. Confident, plain, second person, present tense, sentence case, short sentences.
No emoji. No filler. German grows about thirty per cent, so keep the questions short.
</document_shape>
