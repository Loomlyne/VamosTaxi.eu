<objective>
The rules Phase 7's handler must obey, and the ledger that keeps this phase's open questions
visible after the phase closes.

Two things are worth doing carefully here. The first is that the checkout ladder is where every
promise this phase made either holds or quietly does not: a 30-minute lock that a Worker's clock
can extend, a `pricing_live=false` that a determined caller can talk past, an engine version that
changed under a customer mid-checkout. Writing the ladder in Phase 4 — where the lock, the kernel
and the gates were all built — rather than in Phase 7 next to a Stripe call, is what stops it
becoming five scattered `if`s.

The second is the numbers. ADR-014 answered several the research had left open — 10 % round trip,
180 minutes minimum advance, 30-day manage link, 60/15 waiting, 30/30 clocks — and every one of
them now lives on a versioned row that Phase 2 already seeded. The failure mode is not that they
are missing; it is that one of them gets copied into TypeScript as a "sensible default" and the
row stops being the source of truth. A committed grep gate is cheaper than remembering.

Purpose: hand Phase 7 an ordered ladder, and hand the owner a list of what is still open.
Output: `lib/quote/intent.ts`, the D-49 probe, the Law 04 CI gate, two runbooks, and the recorded
owner answers.
</objective>
